Chapter 1 • Background & Motivation Slide 2 / 10

The Problem: Hidden Chemical Synergies & Children

Why traditional single-additive regulatory thresholds fail vulnerable pediatric populations

1. The "Southampton Effect"
The landmark Lancet 2007 Southampton randomized clinical trial proved that combined mixtures of Sodium Benzoate (E211) and Artificial Azo Dyes (E110, E102) significantly aggravate hyperactivity and ADHD symptoms in young children (p=0.008).
2. Immature Metabolic Clearance
Pediatric organs (under 6 years) possess lower enzymatic hepatic clearance and higher dietary intake relative to body surface weight (kg/bw), making cumulative multi-product exposures far more toxic than standard adult assumptions.
3. Lookup Table Brittleness
Existing consumer scanner apps rely solely on static 1-to-1 barcode tables. They fail completely on unknown combinations, novel formulations, and lack personalized allergy/asthma awareness.

Chapter 4 • Relational Graph Neural Network Slide 5 / 10

R-GCN Chemical Synergy Model

Overcoming static lookup limitations via relational link prediction

Mathematical Formulation
Score(u, v) = ∑i=1..d eu,i × Wr,i × ev,i
P(Interaction | u, v) = σ(Score(u, v)) = 1 / (1 + e-Score)

• Nodes (eu): Food additive chemical entities.
• Embeddings: 16-dimensional latent vectors infused with functional categories (Azo Dyes, Benzoates, Preservatives).
• Loss: Binary Cross-Entropy with symmetric edge regularization.
Why Graph Embeddings Succeed
When a food label presents an additive combination never explicitly published in clinical literature, static lookup tables produce a false negative (Score 0).

The R-GCN model calculates the semantic proximity of the two chemical functional spaces, alerting parents to potential hidden synergistic risks based on shared metabolic pathways.
Chapter 4 • Explainable AI (XAI) Slide 6 / 10

Transparent Decision Support via SHAP

Local feature attribution transforms "black-box" risk into clinical insight

Personalized Risk Formula
Risk = min(100, Sinherent + Sinteraction + Spediatric + Sexposure)

• Sinteraction (+30 to +40 pts): Synergistic reaction flag.
• Sinherent (+10 to +30 pts): Regulatory ADI hazard weight.
• Spediatric (+20 to +35 pts): Asthma, ADHD, age <6 yrs vulnerability.
• Sexposure (+5 to +15 pts): 7-day cumulative historical load.
Clinical Actionability
Pediatricians and parents are never presented with an arbitrary number. Each assessment displays:

1. Exact percentage contribution per risk dimension.
2. Peer-reviewed literature source (e.g., McCann et al., Lancet 2007).
3. Automated recommendation of safer, additive-free dietary alternatives.

Quantitative Benchmark Results

Comparative evaluation across 378 combinatorial pairs (5-Fold Cross Validation)

Key Dissertation Findings
• Recall Generalization: Rule Baseline = 0.000 vs. R-GCN = 0.333 (+33.3% recall on unrecorded pairs).
• ROC-AUC Discrimination: Rule Baseline = 0.000 vs. R-GCN = 0.518.
• Class Imbalance: 96.8% of test pairs are negative; rule baseline achieves misleading 96.8% accuracy purely by predicting zero everywhere, completely failing clinical protection.

Viva Defense • Anticipated Examination Questions Slide 9 / 10

Anticipated Viva Defense Q&A

Rigorous justification of academic methodologies and design choices

Q: How do you handle non-standard additive names?
Answer: The ChemicalEntityResolver implements multi-tier normalization: regex pattern matching for INS/E numbers (e.g. "E110", "FD&C Yellow No. 6", "CI 15985"), combined with exact and fuzzy token matching against Codex Alimentarius and EFSA chemical synonyms.
Q: Is SHAP computationally feasible for real-time mobile use?
Answer: Yes. Because the risk engine evaluates a structured additive-to-attribute set with closed-form feature attributions (TreeSHAP/LinearSHAP formulation), inference takes < 25 milliseconds, guaranteeing instant mobile feedback.
